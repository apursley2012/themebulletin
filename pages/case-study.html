ThemeBulletin

A Gallery Built for Discovery

The thinking, structure, and tradeoffs behind ThemeBulletin.

Project case study · Design and implementation

A home for 64 distinct portfolio ideas

A collection of standalone portfolio websites deserves more than a list of links. ThemeBulletin turns 64 independently published designs into a visual directory that helps visitors discover a direction, understand its character, and explore the original project.

01 · The challenge

Make variety feel navigable

Every theme has its own visual identity, content structure, and published destination. That independence is part of the appeal, but it can make the collection difficult to browse: visitors need to open separate pages before they can see how the designs compare.

The challenge was to create a recognizable point of entry without making the individual projects feel interchangeable. ThemeBulletin needed to be a directory, not another theme layered over the work.

02 · The design response

Use the bulletin board as a wayfinding system

The corkboard gives the collection a playful, memorable visual language. Four categories—Retro Tech & Arcade, Cosmic & Celestial, Colorful & Creative, and Magical & Spooky—help visitors narrow their search by mood and style.

Category boards use pinned notes to present themes in a compact, browsable arrangement. A dedicated profile page then gives each design room for a larger preview, a short introduction, highlights, and clear links to its live demo and source repository.

Underneath the visual treatment, familiar links and buttons keep the journey straightforward: choose a category, select a theme, and decide whether to view the published project or inspect its code.

03 · How it works

One catalog, independent destinations

A central JavaScript dataset holds the information used by the gallery: theme titles, categories, summaries, preview assets, profile paths, and links to live demos and repositories. That shared source supports the category navigation and theme selection experience.

The site itself uses HTML, CSS, and lightweight JavaScript. It is designed to publish as a static website on GitHub Pages, without a database or application server. Each theme profile is also a directly accessible HTML page, so visitors can reach a specific design without navigating through the homepage first.

04 · Decisions and tradeoffs

Keep the visual composition intact

Each square board is arranged as a complete composition. On smaller screens, the board and its pinned contents scale together, preserving their relative placement and proportions. This keeps the design recognizable across devices, though smaller screens naturally reduce the size of the text inside the board.

Long-form pages such as this case study use responsive, reflowing text outside the corkboard. That distinction lets the visual directory keep its character without making extended reading depend on scaled-down text.

05 · The result

Discovery without losing individuality

ThemeBulletin brings the collection into one place while leaving each project free to stand on its own. Visitors can browse by creative direction, compare first impressions, and follow a consistent path from preview to live experience or source code.

The directory is an introduction, not a substitute for exploring the themes themselves. Screenshots communicate a visual direction; the published sites and repositories provide the fuller picture.
